# Viết Case Study

## When to Use This Skill

Use this skill when:
- A user wants to turn a completed project or client engagement into a case study
- Someone needs social proof for their website, proposals, sales decks, or pitch materials
- A user says they want to "write up a client win" or "showcase results"
- A freelancer, agency, or consultant wants to document project success stories
- A user needs a one-pager or short-form case study for a specific sales conversation

**DO NOT** use this skill for:
- Testimonials or reviews (those are client quotes, not structured narratives)
- Portfolio pieces without measurable results (use a portfolio skill instead)
- Internal project retrospectives or post-mortems (those are for the team, not prospects)
- Product feature announcements (those are marketing copy, not case studies)

---

## Core Principle

A CASE STUDY IS A PROOF ENGINE, NOT A PROJECT SUMMARY. EVERY SECTION MUST MOVE THE READER FROM "THAT SOUNDS NICE" TO "I WANT THOSE RESULTS FOR MY BUSINESS." IF THERE ARE NO NUMBERS, THERE IS NO CASE STUDY.

---

## Phase 1: Interview

Conduct a structured conversational interview to extract everything needed. **Do not skip this phase.**

### Group 1 -- Client and Context (ask all at once):

1. Who is the client? (Company name, industry, company size or stage)
2. What is their business model? (B2B, B2C, e-commerce, service-based, SaaS)
3. Do we have permission to use their name and logo? (Yes / No / Need to ask)
4. Who was the main point of contact? (Name, role)

### Group 2 -- The Problem (ask after Group 1 is answered):

5. What was the client's situation before working with you? (The "before" picture)
6. What specific problem were they trying to solve? (In their words if possible)
7. What had they already tried? (Previous solutions, vendors, internal attempts that failed)
8. What was the business impact of the problem? (Lost revenue, wasted time, missed opportunities -- get numbers)

### Group 3 -- The Solution and Results (ask after Group 2 is answered):

9. What did you deliver? (Specific services, products, deliverables)
10. Walk me through the process -- what were the key steps or phases?
11. What was the timeline from start to completion?
12. What were the measurable results? (Revenue, leads, time saved, cost reduced, conversion rates -- every number you have)
13. Were there any unexpected wins or secondary benefits?
14. Do you have a direct quote from the client? (Even paraphrased is useful)
15. What is the one result you are most proud of from this project?

### Extraction Summary

After the interview, present a structured summary:

```
**Client:** Greenfield Hotels (Hospitality, 12 locations)
**Permission:** Name approved, logo pending
**Contact:** Rachel Torres, VP of Marketing
**Before:** Managing social media in-house with a 1-person team.
Posting inconsistently across 12 locations, no content calendar,
no performance tracking.
**Problem:** Inconsistent brand voice, no system for scaling content,
coordinator stretched too thin. Previous freelancer delivered generic
posts that did not match the brand.
**Solution:** Social media management system for all 12 locations --
content calendar, brand voice guide, templated frameworks, reporting
dashboard, and 3 months of done-for-you content.
**Timeline:** 8 weeks build + 3 months managed execution
**Results:**
- Social engagement up 215% across all locations
- 340 direct booking inquiries from social in 3 months
- Content production time: 20 hrs/week down to 6 hrs/week
- Posting cadence: 4x/week per location (up from 2-3x)
**Quote:** "We finally look like a unified brand instead of 12
separate hotels posting whatever they feel like." -- Rachel Torres
**Proudest result:** 340 booking inquiries -- direct trackable revenue
```

**GATE: Do not proceed to Phase 2 until you have confirmed: (1) a specific problem, (2) a specific solution, and (3) at least one measurable result with a real number.**

---

## Phase 2: Structure

### Case Study Framework

1. **HEADLINE** -- [Result] for [Client Type] in [Timeframe]
2. **SNAPSHOT BOX** -- 3-4 key metrics in a scannable table
3. **CHALLENGE** -- The "before" picture with pain points, failed attempts, business impact
4. **SOLUTION** -- What was done, step by step, with specifics
5. **RESULTS** -- Every metric quantified with before/after comparisons
6. **TESTIMONIAL QUOTE** -- Pull quote from the client
7. **CALL TO ACTION** -- Connects to the user's service offering

### Format Selection

**Default to long-form unless the user specifies otherwise.**

| Format | Word Count | Best For |
|--------|-----------|----------|
| **Long-form** (default) | 800-1,200 words | Website pages, proposals, sales enablement |
| **Short-form** | 300-500 words | Social media, email campaigns, pitch decks |
| **One-pager** | 200-300 words | Leave-behinds, meeting handouts, PDF attachments |

**GATE: Do not proceed to Phase 3 until the user approves the structure.**

---

## Phase 3: Write

### Headline

Formula: **"[Result] for [Client Type] in [Timeframe]"** -- must contain a number.

Strong: "215% Social Engagement Increase for a 12-Location Hotel Chain in 3 Months"
Weak: "How We Helped Greenfield Hotels Succeed on Social Media"

### Snapshot Box

3-4 key metrics immediately after the headline, each with a number, in before/after format.

### Challenge Section

1. Set the scene -- who is the client and their situation (2-3 sentences)
2. Name the specific problem (1-2 sentences)
3. Show what they tried and why it failed (1-2 sentences)
4. Quantify the business impact (1-2 sentences with numbers)

### Solution Section

1. High-level approach in one sentence
2. Step-by-step breakdown with named phases and timeframes
3. Key decisions that made the approach work

### Results Section

Every metric gets a before/after comparison. Format:

```
**Social Engagement: +215%**
Average rate across all 12 locations went from 1.2% to 3.8%.

**Booking Inquiries: 340 in 3 months**
First time tracking social-to-booking attribution via UTM codes.
```

**EVERY result must have a number.**

### Testimonial Quote

```
> "We finally look like a unified brand instead of 12 separate
> hotels posting whatever they feel like."
>
> -- **Rachel Torres**, VP of Marketing, Greenfield Hotels
```

**NEVER fabricate quotes.** Skip the section if no quote exists.

### Call to Action

One CTA connecting the case study results to the user's service. Use `[your-link-here]` if no link provided.

---

## Phase 4: Deliver

1. Write the complete case study
2. Confirm word count and result count
3. Offer short-form derivative for emails, social, or pitch decks
4. Suggest placements: website, proposals, LinkedIn, pitch decks, PDF leave-behind

---

## Anti-Patterns

- **DO NOT include results without numbers.**
- **DO NOT use the client's name without explicit permission.**
- **DO NOT write vague solutions.** Specify deliverables, frameworks, and quantities.
- **DO NOT skip the "before" picture.**
- **DO NOT fabricate or embellish results.**
- **DO NOT bury results.** Snapshot box goes immediately after the headline.
- **DO NOT exceed word limits.** 1,200 max for long-form, 500 for short-form.

---

## Recovery

### No Measurable Results Available
After 3 attempts with no numbers: "A case study without measurable results will not be persuasive. Would you like me to draft a quick email requesting the data from your client?"

### Client Wants to Remain Anonymous
Replace company name with a descriptor and contact name with role. Keep all metrics and quotes attributed to role, not name.

### Vague Problem Description
Ask: "What was happening in their business because of this?" and "What triggered them to reach out?"

### Three Revision Attempts Fail
"Can you point to a case study you have seen with the tone or structure you want? I can model the next draft on it."

---

## Pre-Delivery Checklist

- [ ] Headline contains a number and follows "[Result] for [Client Type] in [Timeframe]"
- [ ] Snapshot box has 3-4 metrics, each with a number
- [ ] Challenge includes a failed previous attempt or quantified pain point
- [ ] Solution is broken into named phases with timeframes
- [ ] Every result has a before/after number
- [ ] Client quote is real (not fabricated) and properly attributed
- [ ] No vague language ("improved," "enhanced") without a number
- [ ] Word count within format range
- [ ] Written in third person (except CTA)
- [ ] No irrelevant project details
