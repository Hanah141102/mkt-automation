# Tin Tuyển Dụng

## When to Use This Skill

Use this skill when:
- A user needs to write a job description for a full-time, part-time, or contract role
- Someone is hiring their first employee and does not know how to structure a posting
- A user wants to post a role on job boards (Indeed, LinkedIn, We Work Remotely, Upwork)
- An entrepreneur needs a careers-page description for their website

**DO NOT** use this skill for internal role documentation, contractor agreements, or performance reviews.

---

## Core Principle

THE TITLE IS THE MOST IMPORTANT LINE — IT DETERMINES SEARCH VISIBILITY. EVERY OTHER ELEMENT EITHER EARNS THE NEXT READ OR LOSES THE CANDIDATE.

---

## Job Post Structure

Every post follows this 7-section structure.

### 1. Title

**Rules:**
- Use the industry-standard title candidates actually search for
- Include seniority level (Junior, Mid-Level, Senior, Lead)
- Include specialization (Frontend, Content, Paid Media, Operations)
- Never use internal jargon or inflated titles (no "Ninja," "Rockstar," "Guru")
- Keep under 60 characters for full visibility on mobile

### 2. Opening Hook (2-3 sentences)

Sell the opportunity, not the company. Answer: "Why should I care about this role?"

Structure: (1) Impact this person will make, (2) why the timing matters, (3) one differentiator (autonomy, ownership, specific challenge).

### 3. About the Company (3-4 sentences)

1. What the company does in plain language
2. Company stage or size
3. One specific proof point (revenue, customers, funding, growth metric)
4. Who they would work with

### 4. What You Will Do (5-7 responsibilities)

**Rules:**
- Start every bullet with a strong action verb (Build, Own, Lead, Design, Analyze)
- Pair the action with a measurable outcome or context
- Order from highest-impact to lowest-impact
- NEVER write more than 7 bullets

### 5. What You Bring

Split into two clear groups:

**Must-Have (3-5 items):** Every item must be genuinely required to do the job on day one. Specific and verifiable — not vague personality traits.

**Nice-to-Have (2-3 items):** Accelerators, not dealbreakers. Frame as "bonus" or "a plus."

### 6. What We Offer

Be specific. Include:
- Compensation range (required — postings without pay ranges get fewer applicants)
- Health/dental/vision (state contribution level)
- PTO and flexibility
- Remote/hybrid/on-site details
- Growth trajectory (promotion path, learning budget)
- Equity or profit-sharing if applicable

### 7. How to Apply

Include: exactly what to submit, where to submit it, timeline for response, and an optional brief prompt replacing the cover letter.

## Role Type Adaptations

| Element | Full-Time Employee | Freelancer / Contractor | Part-Time |
|---------|-------------------|------------------------|-----------|
| **Title** | Standard title + seniority | Add "Freelance" or "Contract" prefix | Add "Part-Time" prefix |
| **Hook** | Career opportunity framing | Project scope and impact framing | Flexibility + specific hours |
| **Responsibilities** | 5-7 ongoing | 3-5 project deliverables with deadlines | 3-5 scoped to weekly hours |
| **Requirements** | Must-Have (3-5) + Nice-to-Have (2-3) | Must-Have (2-3) + portfolio requirement | Must-Have (2-3) |
| **Compensation** | Salary range + benefits + equity | Project fee or hourly rate + payment terms | Hourly rate + estimated hours/week |

---

## Pre-Delivery Checklist

- [ ] Title is searchable and matches industry-standard naming
- [ ] Opening hook focuses on impact and opportunity
- [ ] Company section has at least one specific proof point
- [ ] Responsibilities are 5-7 bullets, each with an action verb and outcome
- [ ] Requirements split into Must-Have (3-5) and Nice-to-Have (2-3)
- [ ] No gendered language (rockstar, ninja, guru, guys, he/she)
- [ ] Compensation range is included
- [ ] Benefits are specific (dollar amounts, percentages, specific policies)
- [ ] Application instructions include what to submit, where, and timeline
- [ ] Remote/hybrid/on-site is stated clearly
- [ ] No internal jargon or unexplained acronyms
- [ ] Equal opportunity statement included at the bottom

---

## Anti-Patterns

- **Gendered or exclusionary language** — no "rockstar," "ninja," "guru," "manpower." Use "you" and "they."
- **More than 5 must-have requirements** — long lists deter qualified candidates.
- **Hidden compensation** — "Competitive salary" means nothing. State a range.
- **Responsibilities as a task laundry list** — pair every action with an outcome.
- **"Other duties as assigned"** — signals an undefined role.
- **10+ years for a mid-level role** — match experience demands to actual seniority and pay.
- **Walls of text** — use headers, short bullets, and line breaks.

---

## Recovery

**User does not know the job title:** Ask what the person will spend most of their time doing. Map to a standard title.

**User cannot define responsibilities:** "Walk me through a typical week for this person." If more than 7 areas surface, flag it: "This sounds like two roles."

**User wants to skip compensation:** "Postings with salary ranges get significantly more qualified applicants." If they refuse, use "Compensation discussed during interview process."

**User describes 3 jobs in one:** "Which function is the most urgent?" If they insist, acknowledge breadth in the hook: "This is a generalist role for someone who thrives wearing multiple hats."

**GATE: Do not save to file until the user explicitly approves the posting.**
