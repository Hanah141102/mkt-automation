# Nội Quy Nơi Làm Việc

## When to Use This Skill

Use this skill when you need to:
- Create workplace policies for a small or growing team
- Establish remote work guidelines, communication norms, and conduct expectations
- Document equipment, time-off, and professional development policies
- Build a lightweight employee handbook for a startup or small business

**DO NOT** use this skill for full HR compliance manuals, employment contracts, or legal compliance in regulated industries. Consult HR counsel for legal compliance requirements.

---

## Core Principle

WORKPLACE POLICIES SHOULD EMPOWER, NOT MICROMANAGE — CLEAR EXPECTATIONS WITH REASONABLE FLEXIBILITY ATTRACT AND RETAIN GREAT PEOPLE.

---

## Phase 1: Company Context

### Required Inputs

| Input | What to Ask | Default |
|-------|------------|---------|
| **Company name** | "What is your company name?" | No default — must be provided |
| **Team size** | "How many people are on your team?" | 2-10 |
| **Work arrangement** | "Remote, hybrid, or in-office?" | Remote |
| **Team type** | "Employees, contractors, or mix?" | Mix |
| **Policies needed** | "Which policies do you need? (remote work, communication, conduct, equipment, PTO)" | All core policies |
| **Company values** | "What are 2-3 core values that should shape the policies?" | Autonomy, transparency, results |

**GATE: Do not proceed without company name, team size, and work arrangement.**

---

## Phase 2: Core Policies

### Remote Work Policy

```
## Remote Work Policy

**Work Location:** Team members may work from any location with reliable internet connectivity.

**Core hours:** [10am-3pm in team's primary timezone] — be available for meetings and collaboration
**Flexible hours:** Remaining hours are flexible — manage your schedule around deliverables
**Expected weekly hours:** [40] hours for full-time team members

**Communication:**
- Respond to messages within [2-4] hours during core hours
- Update your status when unavailable for 30+ minutes
- Time zone awareness — do not schedule meetings outside someone's reasonable work hours without asking

**Home Office:** [Company provides / reimburses up to $X for] home office setup. Reliable internet connection required (minimum [25] Mbps recommended).
```

### Communication Policy

```
## Communication Policy

| Channel | Use For | Response Time |
|---------|---------|--------------|
| [Slack/Teams] | Day-to-day communication, quick questions | 2-4 hours |
| Email | External communication, formal requests | 24 hours |
| Video calls | Meetings, complex discussions, 1:1s | Scheduled |
| [Project tool] | Task updates, project discussions | Same business day |
| Phone/text | Urgent issues only | ASAP |

**Meeting Culture:**
- Default meeting length: 25 or 50 minutes
- All meetings have an agenda shared in advance
- No-meeting blocks: [e.g., Tuesday and Thursday mornings]
- If a meeting can be an async update, make it one
- Meeting notes shared within 24 hours

**Async-First:** Write things down instead of scheduling a call when possible. Respect focus time.
```

### Professional Conduct

```
## Professional Conduct Policy

**Core Expectations:** Treat colleagues, clients, and partners with respect and professionalism. Communicate honestly. Meet commitments or communicate proactively when you cannot. Protect confidential information.

**Anti-Harassment:** Zero-tolerance policy for harassment, discrimination, or bullying based on any protected characteristic. Report concerns to [designated person / HR contact].

**Social Media:** Do not share confidential company information. If posting about [Company] or industry topics, note that views are your own unless authorized to speak for the company.

**Conflict Resolution:**
1. Address directly with the person involved
2. If unresolved, involve your manager or team lead
3. If still unresolved, escalate to [founder / HR / designated person]
```

### Equipment and Tools

```
## Equipment Policy

**Company-Provided:**
- Laptop/computer: [provided / $X stipend]
- Software licenses: All required tools provided
- Home office stipend: $[X] one-time / $[X] monthly

**Security:**
- Two-factor authentication required on all accounts
- Devices must have screen locks enabled

**Return of Equipment:** All company equipment must be returned within [14] days of termination.
```

### Time Off

```
## Time Off Policy

**PTO:** [X] days per year / Unlimited with [X]-day minimum encouraged
**Request notice:** At least [1 week] in advance for planned absences
**Sick Leave:** [X] sick days per year. No doctor's note required for 3 or fewer consecutive days.
**Holidays:** [List company-observed holidays]
**Bereavement:** [3-5] days for immediate family
**Parental Leave:** [X weeks paid per company policy]
```

## Phase 3: Customize and Review

- Adjust policies based on team type (employees vs. contractors have different rules)
- Ensure compliance with applicable state and federal labor laws
- Align policies with company values and culture
- Remove sections that do not apply to your business

---

## Phase 4: Delivery

```
## Policy Rollout Checklist

- [ ] All policies reviewed by legal counsel
- [ ] Policies compiled into a single document or handbook
- [ ] Shared with all current team members
- [ ] Included in onboarding materials for new hires
- [ ] Acknowledgment form signed by each team member
- [ ] Annual review date set
- [ ] Stored in accessible shared location
```

---

## Anti-Patterns

- **Over-engineering for a small team** — a 5-person startup does not need a 50-page handbook.
- **Policies that contradict culture** — if you value autonomy but require hourly tracking, the policy undermines trust.
- **No enforcement** — apply rules equally to everyone, including founders.
- **Copying big-company policies** — enterprise policies do not fit startups.
- **Never updating** — review annually or when the team grows significantly.

---

## Recovery

- **No policies and team is growing:** Start with communication norms and conduct expectations — these are the most urgent.
- **Team member violating unwritten rules:** That is a sign you need a written policy. Document the expectation and share it with the full team.
- **Contractor vs. employee confusion:** Contractors should not be subject to most workplace policies — this can trigger misclassification.
- **Policies feel too corporate:** Rewrite in your brand voice. Policies should sound like your company.
